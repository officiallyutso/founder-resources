The board has approved 8,500 tons of greenfield capacity (about Rs 120 crore) plus a further 3,250 tons (Rs 31 crore), taking nameplate from 29,000 tons to 40,750 tons and piece weight from 2.5 tons to about 3.5 tons. Tamboli said an induction furnace "does very limited refining," customers have been asking for an electric arc furnace of about 6 to 6.5 tons, and the company keeps "regretting 400, 500 tons of business" a year in the 2.5 to 3.5 ton gap. Renewable plan as stated: 9.5 MW already commissioned, plus 2.4 MW hybrid and 1.4 MW solar aimed at 31 December 2026, "expected to generate annual power cost savings of approximately INR 4.5 crores." That saving is a company expectation, not a measured result.

Manufacture of Fabricated Metal Products is 7.72%. August's final all-commodity index was left unchanged at 9.92%. The October gas figure is a year-on-year index movement. ## 3. Learning

Three free pieces, three sources, inside 6 to 20 November. None is the 16 November Saloni Dattani column already used on 18 November, and none is the 12 November Potter essay used on 16 November.

**1. The Evolving Story of How Chemistry Became Biology.** Yasemin Saplakoglu, Quanta Magazine, 12 November 2026. Reading time not printed; the essay is long, about 20 minutes. https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-evolving-story-of-how-chemistry-became-biology-20261112/

Central idea. After decades of arguing which molecule came first (RNA, lipids, proteins, metabolites), a newer group has dropped the single-candidate rule. Precursors look easier to form on an early Earth than the textbooks suggested. What is harder is how those processes became one system that stores information both reliably and imperfectly enough to keep a lineage going. Biologists are working backward from living cells. Chemists are working forward from odd mixtures. Synthetic biologists are trying systems that may never have existed.

What to carry. A plant argument that starts by crowning one cause (the furnace, the steel, the operator) stalls the same way. The useful question is which records have to stay attached to each other for the lineage of a lot to remain checkable.

This is a different Quanta piece from the 10 November lipid essay. Overlap of topic is real. The move in this one is the refusal of a single first molecule.

**2. The edge of biotech hedge funds.** Abhishaike Mahajan, Works in Progress, 19 November 2026. Reading time not printed; a column, about 12 minutes. https://worksinprogress.co/issue/how-do-biotech-hedge-funds-make-money/

Central idea. A lot of trial outcomes are predictable from public design choices, and he cites an Insilico preprint claim of 79% accuracy on 14 resolved Phase II trials. He then asks why that does not erase the funds. His answer is paid access to people who have seen the trial up close, illustrated by the Martoma case (a convicted insider-trading prosecution, not a how-to). The legal consultations, he says, continue in a narrower form. He also credits long public conviction, Baker Brothers in Seagen, so the piece is not only a scandal note.

What to carry. A model on public data and a conversation with the person who runs the process are different instruments. Price the second one honestly. Do not romanticise the first.

**3. How Do Building Codes Affect the Price of Housing?** Brian Potter, Construction Physics, 6 November 2026. Reading time not printed; about 15 minutes. https://www.construction-physics.com/p/how-do-building-codes-affect-the

The 7 November reading list on the same site says this essay was first emailed to paid subscribers by mistake and then opened. The full essay loaded on this fetch, so it is free as checked this morning.

Central idea. He separates land-use rules from the cost of the building code itself. Using NAHB's code-change costing, he writes that IRC changes since 2006 have added about $40,000 to a reference new home, about 0.5% a year, against a Census construction cost he puts near $440,000 for 2024. An Eriksen, Besiktepe and Martani paper on IBC changes from 2012 to 2021 finds a 9.9% cumulative cost on three-storey apartments, about 0.8% a year, with a large share from a few provisions (one fire-watch rule at about $180,000 of roughly $400,000). NAHB is a builders' lobby, which he flags. Several safety and energy provisions have published benefit-cost ratios above 1. A two-stair apartment rule is his example of cost with a thin safety case (a 2025 Center for Building in North America range of 6 to 13% of building cost). He also notes that construction price indexes often miss code-driven quality, unlike the vehicle index.

What to carry. When a standard looks expensive, ask whether a handful of clauses carry the cost. A heat-treat audit with forty lines often fails on three of them.
