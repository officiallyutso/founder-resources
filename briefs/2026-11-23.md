### Lead: Alicon's record quarter is three different sizes

**Title:** Alicon Castalloy Limited, Q2 and H1 FY27 earnings call transcript.
**Source:** Exchange filing. Call hosted with CDR India. Speakers named on the call: Sumit Bhatnagar, Group CEO, and Vimal Gupta, Group CFO. Rajiv Gupta also answers and is not given a title in the opening.
**Original date:** Call Monday 16 November 2026, 11:30 AM IST. Transcript filed with BSE and NSE on 20 November 2026. I read the transcript PDF, not the audio.
**Reading time:** About 12 minutes for the CEO and CFO openings (through the margin bridge). The full transcript is 29 pages, closer to 35 minutes.
**URL:** https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ALICON_20112026112608_Letter_to_SEs_for_Transcript.pdf

**What happened.** Alicon, an aluminium caster with plants in Pune, at Binola in Gurugram district, and in Slovakia, reported total income of Rs 652 crore for Q2 FY27. Bhatnagar called it the highest quarter in the company's history: up 50% year on year and 12.6% on the previous quarter. Gupta's figure for the same line is 50.4% year on year.

On the same call they publish two smaller growth rates.

- After alloy price increases, revenue grew 24% year on year and 11.3% sequentially.
- Tonnage rose about 15% sequentially and 19.3% year on year.
- A value-addition metric rose 22% year on year.
- Consolidated EBITDA was Rs 65.3 crore, up 14% year on year. Reported EBITDA margin was 10%, against 9.5% in the previous quarter. Adjusting for higher aluminium prices, Gupta puts the margin at 12.1%.
- H1 total income was Rs 1,231 crore, up 44%. H1 EBITDA margin was 9.8%.
- Cylinder-head share in Indian commercial vehicles moved from about 13% in FY26 to 23% in H1 FY27. Management put the machining share at about 68%.
- Gupta said aluminium pass-through in the half was 100%, and that he expects aluminium down roughly 20% to 25% from September to December. That drop is his expectation, not a settlement print.
- Bhatnagar, referring to Gupta, said the sale increase that came from the alloy increase is not what they call true growth. Full-year guidance is 12% to 15% material-adjusted, not the 44% H1 rupee growth.
- Gupta said the second-half cost work covers conversion cost, energy consumption, tooling, rejections, rework and manpower.

**Evidence quality:** Company-reported. The transcript is an exchange filing of a recorded call. It is not an independent audit of tonnes or of the alloy adjustment. The method behind "alloy-adjusted" is theirs.

**Why it matters.** The wedge is drift that becomes scrap, and scrap is counted in pieces and kilograms. A 50% rupee quarter can be a 19% tonne quarter. Energy per good casting, rejection weight and furnace hours move with the tonnes. The P&L moves with the rupees, and a pass-through of expensive aluminium makes the reported margin look worse (10%) than the alloy-adjusted margin (12.1%). If Gupta's price decline arrives, the next quarter can show slower rupee growth while the furnaces are busier. Staffing, die capacity and the rejection budget should follow the tonnes.

**Post thesis.** A record quarter can be three sizes at once: 50% in rupees, 24% once alloy is removed, and 19% in metal poured. The furnace did the third one. Quote their own refusal to call the alloy increase true growth, then stop. Do not add a product, a client, or a savings figure.

**Ready X post** (158 characters), no tag:

> Alicon's best quarter: revenue up 50%, tonnes up 19%. Management says the alloy increase is not true growth. The furnace counts tonnes. The P&L counts rupees.

**Suggested time:** Today, Monday 23 November, 12:30 to 1:30 PM IST. The transcript has been public since Friday. No tag. Tagging Bhatnagar asks him to defend a distinction he already made.

**Who else might be tagged, and why not yet.** Alicon's company page, only if a later reply is worth having. Binola is one of four plants, so a Gurugram tag overclaims the geography.

**Limitations, check before posting.**

- The 26-point gap between 50.4% and 24% is their alloy adjustment. The further gap between 24% and 19.3% is mix and value-add, which they also claim (value addition +22%). Do not say the entire gap is the aluminium price.
- Consolidated figures include Slovakia, which they describe as still recovering. Binola did not, on this call, get its own number.
- "100% pass-through" and the 20% to 25% price decline are management statements.
- FY26 total income in the annual report was Rs 1,784.47 crore, above the Rs 200 to 1,000 crore prospect band. This is a content item, not a prospect.
- A useful counterpoint sits just outside the window. Happy Forgings' 6 November call, transcript filed 13 November, split the other way. The CFO: finished-goods volume +24.8%, realisation +6.3%, revenue from operations Rs 501 crore (+32.7%), EBITDA margin 30.6%. Ashish Garg put the quarter's realisation near Rs 267 a kilogram. A forge and a foundry can both post a strong quarter and mean different things by growth. Do not present these numbers as this week's news. https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/9876964720_13112026122617_TranscriptEarningscall13112026.pdf

## Section 3. General learning

Three free pieces, three outlets, none of them Quanta, Asterisk, Works in Progress or Abhishaike Mahajan.

**1. The search for the AI-native firm**
David Peterson, Angular Ventures, The Angle Issue 327. 17 November 2026. About 12 minutes. https://newsletter.angularventures.com/p/the-search-for-the-ai-native-firm
Adoption of a chat box is fast. Redesigning the firm around it is slow. Peterson walks through selling software, buying services firms and bolting models on, and building an "AI-native" services firm from scratch. Each one stalls for a different reason. He then borrows Goldratt: throughput is set by the constraint. Automate 90% of the admin and the licensed person, or the furnace, or the owner who must sign the action, is still the limit. The bottleneck moves. It does not disappear. Carry this into any plant tool. A faster chart that nobody owns does not change the tonne of scrap. Ignore the newsletter's aside on Nvidia's leadership and on an Anthropic IPO. Those are not the argument, and I did not verify them separately.

**2. Hardware Is Software**
Tomas Pueyo, Uncharted Territories. 18 November 2026. About 15 minutes. Free on the page I opened. https://unchartedterritories.tomaspueyo.com/p/hardware-is-software
Blake Scholl's line, quoted at length, is the part worth keeping: most hardware engineering is a pile of one-off spreadsheet calculations, so "don't design the hardware, design the algorithm for designing the hardware," and never design a part you could not redesign tomorrow under different assumptions. Pueyo then stacks a much larger claim, on the order of a 65% to 75% cost reduction across a manufacturing chain, humanoid robots in a few years, and a slower school that thinks the same change takes five to twenty years because atoms still have to be tested. Keep Scholl. Treat the 65% to 75% as Pueyo's model. A process recipe is an algorithm. A heat still has to be run before the recipe is true. The piece also asserts a change at the top of Nvidia. I did not verify that, and it is not needed for the essay.

**3. When company culture and business strategy lock arms**
PwC Katzenbach Center. 20 November 2026. The page labels it a 12-minute read. https://www.pwc.com/us/en/services/consulting/human-resources/katzenbach-culture-strategy-alignment-study.html
A study of 466 Fortune 500 companies. Culture traits were read from employee reviews. Strategy was read from filings and CEO statements. Companies whose culture matched the way they say they compete were 2.5 times as likely to land in the top quartile of peer performance. The industrial section is the one to mark. Customers want parts that arrive, work and can be fixed, which rewards discipline, quality and root cause. Digitising the same plants asks for experiments that are allowed to fail. PwC says discipline without a customer in view performs poorly, and that innovation without rigor shows up as a missed delivery or a safety failure. The 2.5 times is an association in their scoring, not a law. The mental model: write down which behaviour is non-negotiable on a safety-critical dimension, and which behaviour is allowed to change. A plant that treats every dimension as both will either freeze or ship the experiment.
